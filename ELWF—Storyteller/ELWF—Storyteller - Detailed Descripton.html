People with the ELWF ("Storyteller") type are natural romantics whose inner universe is woven from deep emotions and an extraordinary sensitivity to the world. In a trusting conversation, a Storyteller opens up almost instantly. They can speak with fascination about the smallest details of life, and their true mood is almost always written across their face.

The greatest strength of ELWF is emotional expressiveness. When they feel ignored, they rarely defend their rights calmly and directly. Instead, they instinctively switch to the language of powerful emotions. To like-minded people, this emotional outburst feels like a natural cry for help, but in unfamiliar environments their tears or raised voice are often mistaken for selfishness or childishness. There is some truth to this: when absorbed in their own emotional drama, the Storyteller can temporarily lose sight of other people's needs.

The most painful paradox of this type lies in self-identity. ELWF often has great difficulty understanding what they truly want. They may spend hours choosing between simple everyday options without reaching a decision. On a larger scale, the combination of emotional overload and chronic indecision can turn life into a long, exhausting, and often frustrating search for their true self.

One undeniable strength of ELWF is their remarkable command of language. They are often highly educated people who can express themselves with sophistication, persuasion, and well-constructed arguments. In intellectual discussions and debates, the Storyteller can be exceptionally impressive, especially when the subject touches on something personally meaningful. The only challenge is that, because of their inner uncertainty, they often need to discover what truly matters to them before they can wholeheartedly defend it.

On the other hand, the material world remains largely unexplored territory. Physical engagement with everyday life—whether sports, cooking, or even ordinary shopping—often feels exhausting. Even simple practical choices may leave them confused, because it is genuinely difficult for them to determine what they actually like. Deep down, they hope that someone else will quietly take care of these practical matters, while doing so gently enough not to hurt their pride.

This situation is made even more complicated by their intense focus on their own emotional experience. As a result, an ELWF may unexpectedly create a dramatic scene at their own birthday celebration by reacting to a gift with something like, "Why did you bring me this? I didn't want it! I have no idea what I actually want—but I know this isn't it!"

Ultimately, many attempts by the Storyteller to make firm, decisive choices end in self-sabotage. In everyday life, this type is often recognizable by a practical detail: they are frequently seen as thoughtful, intellectually sophisticated, and emotionally intense people who wear worn clothing or appear slightly careless about their appearance. Those around them usually learn to accept them as they are and forgive this everyday impracticality.
